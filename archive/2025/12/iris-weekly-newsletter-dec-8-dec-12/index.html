Color- Changing flower Experiment

A few weeks ago, our children asked some questions about leaves, such as: How do leaves drink water? To support their curiosity and promote hands-on learning, we introduced a Color-Changing Flower Experiment.


The children selected different colors: blue, yellow, green, pink, and grey, and we prepared jars with these watercolor paints. Together, the children cut the flower stems and placed the flowers into the different jars. Before beginning, they shared their predictions about how the flowers might change. Many children believed that the flowers in the jars would change to the color of the jars, while others predicted that some flowers might even become rainbow-colored. 


They were amazed to discover that after just one hour, the flower in the blue jar had already begun to show hints of blue on its petals. By the next day, many of the flowers had changed color even more. The children observed these transformations closely and recorded the changes through another set of observational drawings.


From the experiment, our children learnt that flowers and plants drink water through their roots. in cutting flowers, since there are no roots, water travels from the cut directly into the stems and travels to the petals and other parts ofthe  plant.

Exploring Chinese Characters

This week, our children began exploring Chinese characters using different types of sticks. The teacher set out a variety of sticks along with several Chinese characters on the table. Some children quickly noticed that the sticks were different lengths, and they also observed that the character “三” (sān, three) is made up of lines of varying lengths. When learning the character “一” (yī, one), they excitedly raced to build the longest “一.”

Through this activity, the children are developing important skills, such as understanding the placement and proportion of each stroke in a character, and recognizing how Chinese characters are constructed using simple lines.
