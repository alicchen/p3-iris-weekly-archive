Mid-autumn Festival

This week, we continued to celebrate one of the most important traditional Chinese festivals — the Mid-Autumn Festival (Zhōng qiū jié, 中秋节). It is a time to give thanks for the harvest, family reunion, and the full moon 🌕. The festival is also connected to the legend of Chang’e, which tells the story behind the celebration and explains why people honor the moon during this time. During our meeting, we introduced some of the traditional foods enjoyed during the Mid-Autumn Festival, such as mooncakes ( Yuèbǐng, 月饼）and pomelo （柚子，Yòuzǐ). Our children also taste the pomelo and try Pomelo Hat
Ice Mooncake

This week, we were delighted to have Rose and Tammy visit our class and help the children make mooncakes! We used the scale that the children had previously learned about with WenJian Laoshi to measure the amount of filling and outer layer. Through this process, the children practiced comparing objects and developed early math and science thinking. They used words such as heavier, lighter, and equal, made predictions, and tested their ideas through hands-on exploration. By adding or removing objects, they observed how the scale moved and learned how to find balance.

We also introduced the ingredients used to make mooncakes.
For the filling, we made green bean paste.
For the outer layer, we used:

  • Glutinous rice flour (Nuòmǐ fěn, 糯米粉) – for a chewy texture

  • Rice flour (Zhān mǐ fěn, 粘米粉) – to balance the stickiness

  • Sugar (Táng, 糖) – for sweetness

  • Milk or coconut milk (Niú nǎi, 牛奶) – for flavor and moisture

  • Vegetable oil (Cài yóu, 菜油) – for softness
