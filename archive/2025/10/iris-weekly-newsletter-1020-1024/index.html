Observational drawing of Autumn Leaves

During outdoor time, our children noticed that many leaves had fallen on the ground. They also observed that some of the leaves had changed color. The children shared that it is autumn, which is why the leaves turn different colors: some yellow, some red, some half yellow and half green. Our children are also very excited to shared, “Look at these ( leaves)! They are so beautiful!”  " I smell Rose Mary." " I hear crunchy, crunchy sound." " It is soft, I can feel it!" 

 Our children also collected a variety of leaves. Back in the classroom, they explored the leaves more closely by touching, smelling, and observing them with magnifiers. 

 Our children also noticed that each leaf has many lines, some very clear and others faint, and learned that these lines are called veins, which help the leaf carry water and nutrients.

Building on their observations, the children engaged in observational drawing of the autumn leaves. They carefully looked at each leaf’s shape, color, and patterns of veins, trying to represent these details in their artwork. Through this experience, they developed their observation skills, attention to detail, and appreciation for the beauty of nature during the autumn season.

Autumn & leaves

As the children observed the leaves falling from the trees, they shared their understanding that this happens because it is the fall season. They also talked about the Chinese word for fall, “秋天” (qiū tiān), and what it means. Together, we explored the question: What is qiū tiān (autumn)?

The children noticed that during autumn, the weather becomes cooler and many trees lose their leaves. They observed that the leaves change colors: from green to yellow, orange, red, and brown. The children also learned that autumn is a season of celebrations, such as the Mid-Autumn Festival, Halloween, and Thanksgiving. They were especially surprised to discover that some animals collect food and prepare for winter during this time of year.

On Duty 值日生

To support children’s independence and enhance their self-help skills, we introduced an on-duty activity that includes tasks such as cleaning tables and helping to take out of the nap box from the cabinet. Each group of children takes turns being on duty. To make the experience more formal and engaging, the children also created on-duty ID badges, which they proudly wear while completing their responsibilities.

This activity not only fosters a sense of responsibility and teamwork but also helps children develop confidence and ownership in caring for their classroom environment.
