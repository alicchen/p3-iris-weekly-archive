Measure the scallions

A few weeks ago, we placed green onions in a cup of water and left them near the window. Over time, the children quietly observed the changes. One day, a child excitedly came to the teacher and shared, “葱又长啦!” (“The scallions have grown taller!”) This observation inspired new curiosity. " How long are those scallions now?" The children suggested. “We want to measure the length!” 

Rather than providing a single tool, we invited the children to think about how they might measure. They chose a variety of materials and experimented with different ways to compare length. 


At the same time, the children documented their observations in the journals. They recorded the date, the materials they used for measurement, and how many units of each material were needed. This documentation further supported their emerging understanding of measurement and comparison.
