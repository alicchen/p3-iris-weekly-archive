Seeds had sprouted！

Our children returned from mid-winter break amazed to discover that the tiny seeds we planted had sprouted! They also noticed that some of the plants had grown much bigger during the break. The avocado seed had developed a crack, and a few children observed that the potato sitting on the table had also begun to sprout.

One child asked, “Why does the seed sprout without water?” Another child responded, “The seeds drink water from the air because the air has water.”

Some of the children chose to create observational drawings of the plants, while others were eager to measure how tall the plants had grown.

Make signs for our garden

We were about to make signs for our flower bed so that people would notice what is planted there and help protect the plants. The children shared examples of signs they have seen before, such as stop signs and traffic light signs.

Together, we brainstormed ideas for signs for our flower bed. The children suggested messages like “No Touching,” “Don’t Pull,” “Look with Your Eyes,” and “Remember to Water the Plants.”

One child shared a thoughtful idea, drawing lots of hearts around the plants because “plants need a lot of care and love.” It was a beautiful reminder that signs are not only for setting boundaries, but also for expressing care and responsibility toward living things.
Can the roots be eaten?

We discovered that not all roots can be eaten!

Although the roots are very small, there are so many of them that they spread out and cover the entire soil surface.

With the question in mind, “Can roots be eaten?” the children began sharing their ideas. Some said that we cannot eat roots because they are too hard and dirty. Others disagreed and shared examples: “Ginger is a kind of root we eat.” “We also eat garlic.” “I ate a carrot before.”

To extend their thinking, the teacher brought in a white radish for the children to observe, smell, and touch. We also cut it into small pieces for closer exploration. Some of the children were very sensitive to the strong smell when we cut it.

Afterward, we used the white radish to cook. The children helped mix it with rice flour to make turnip cake. They were more excited about making the food than eating the turnip cake.
