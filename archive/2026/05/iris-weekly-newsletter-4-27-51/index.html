Which condition of seeds will germinate first?

While we were discussing the conditions seeds need in order to grow, many children shared that seeds need sunlight, water, and soil. However, when we explored what might happen if seeds were grown without soil, we decided to use alternative materials instead. We created three different scenarios:

  1. Seeds were provided with only two drops of water

  2. Seeds were provided with two drops of water and covered with a paper towel

  3. Seeds were provided with two drops of water and covered with leaves, as suggested by Oliver

We then asked the question: Which seeds will germinate first?

Our children make a prediction and vote on which scenarios of the seed will germinate first. 


Children choose Scenario 1:

There is more water; plants need water to grow. -Juniper 

没有东西挡住它会先发芽. ( If nothing is blocking, seeds will sprout first.)  -Ari

Nothing covered, so the sunlight will go inside the seed. -Lillian   

There is nothing blocking the container. -Max

Can the seeds of Box 2 will germinate?

After one day, Boxes 1 and 3 germinated first. Box 1 contained seeds provided with only two drops of water, and Box 3 contained seeds provided with two drops of water and covered with leaves.

However, the seeds in Box 2 had not germinated yet. Some of the children thought that the seeds in Box 2 would not germinate because the paper towel had absorbed all the water. Skyler said, “Box 2 needs more time to grow. It needs 10 days!” We then began using stickers to count the days, adding one sticker each day until the seeds germinated.

Some children also noticed that in Box 3, two of the seeds had germinated, but one had not. Ari said, “Because those two seeds drank all the water, there was no water left for the third seed.”

This led to another question: Can a seed germinate without water? To investigate further, we planted another box, Box 4, with seeds placed on only one paper towel.

We will continue to discover more next week!

Discovering Seeds Through Fruit Exploration

We had a fruit and seed tasting activity a few weeks ago, which helped children investigate their questions," Where do seeds come from?" Through hands-on exploration, children discovered that many seeds are found inside fruits. At the same time, this activity supports their understanding through sensory and meaningful experiences.
