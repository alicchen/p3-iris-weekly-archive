How Old Are You?

  Please do not be surprised if your child starts asking about your age or the ages of your friends and neighbors, as we have been having discussions about this topic in class.

Our children's curiosity and wonder about age began in January, after the death of our class shrimp and fish

   We felt sad after losing our class pets and read a book called 'lifetimes, the beautiful way to explain death to children.' We also created a poster in the classroom to document the people and pets who have been close to us and have left us. The teacher noted how children perceive death at their age. 

     We would like to thank you for your openness in discussing this topic. We understand it can be  difficult, but it is also meaningful, as it is a natural part for all living things. As the book says, "for plants, for people. for bird. for fish. for animals. even for the tiniest insects".  We respect every family's cultural and religious backgrounds  and leave further discussion to you at home. 

   We also discover that the oldest person who lived on earth is Jeanne, a French lady. She had a lifetime of 122 years old. 

   When reading a book about PIcaso, we learned that he lived to 92 years old.

   Since the children have been curious about the teachers’ ages, we came up with three questions for them:
First, who is the oldest among the teachers?
Second, how do you know?
Third, can you see age?

The children had a wonderful discussion. Here are some of their ideas:

  • “If you are taller, you are older, so Wenjian Laoshi is the oldest.”
  • “If you have less hair, you are older. So Kungfu Shifu is older than Tina Laoshi.”
  • “If your shoes are bigger, then you are older. So Wenjian Laoshi is older than Christie Laoshi.”
  • “If you have white hair, you are older.” But Ari argued, “I have one white hair. It is hidden inside. My mom told me.”
  • “I think Tina Laoshi’s face has fewer lines than Wenjian Laoshi’s, so Tina is younger.”

I used to think that talking about age was taboo in Western culture. Since children are so interested in it, I think we can break that barrier and talk about it openly.


Connecting Age to Math

Due to the children’s strong interest in age, we started an activity this week called “finding your age on the line.” The teacher asked, “If this line represents 10 years old, where would you place 5 years old or 4 years old (your age) on it? Where would the dots for a 6 years old boy and 9 years old girl go?”

You can see the children’s work displayed. Please do not teach or correct this at home, as the children are exploring it in a holistic way, with their own ideas and stories behind it. We believe that everyone in our class will figure it out sooner or later.

The purpose here, as Reggio Emilia philosophy suggests, is to support children’s thinking by asking them “why” and “how do you know?” or “how can you prove it?”. The strong image that we hold for our children is that they are capable of learning, reasoning, and discovering answers through their own investigations.
